07 / BUY TOGETHER · FUTURE CONCEPT

One demand pool. A price residents can choose.

Resident · optional monthly essentials circle

Interest is not an order. Confirm only after the supplier's final price is shown.

Society lead and approved supplier

A wholesaler or farmer group confirms its quote; sponsorship never selects the winner.

Illustrative workflow only. The society may request two or more comparable quotes and disclose selection conflicts; residents indicate interest before a fixed offer, then explicitly confirm or decline. If minimum demand is not met or the price changes, no silent order is placed. A supplier must agree to household-level billing and fulfillment at the quoted aggregate price; Wooru holds no stock or resident funds. Packing, transport, optional last-mile delivery, cancellations, quality complaints and replacement terms affect net savings. A low farm-gate price is not the final price. Delivery API integration requires validated multi-stop economics and a provider quote; it is not shown as shipped.

Residents and suppliers pay no Wooru fee.

A sponsorship belongs to the association, not Wooru. Wooru earns only when the association signs and pays a separate service agreement.

Primary · society contract

Sign only when sponsor funding and the society's approval can cover the fee.

Later · enterprise contract

A property operator may pay for proven multi-community tools. No buyer or price is validated.

Later · managed support

Separately scope human procurement or campaign assistance; price its staffing cost.

Residents pay the supplier for goods and any chosen delivery. No hidden item margin, personal-data sale or sponsor-controlled supplier ranking.

These are income hypotheses, not current contracts or forecasts. Sponsored placements and procurement decisions need separate approvals, comparable landed-price quotes and disclosed conflicts. A single society may not support meaningful ad spend; a multi-society program is a later opportunity, not assumed revenue. Funding gate: contracted association fee must exceed actual platform, supplier support, communications, sales and operating costs with a sustainable margin. Consumer gate: the final price including packing, transport and any doorstep option must beat a comparable local offer without relying on an undisclosed subsidy. A delivery fee is a separately disclosed fulfillment cost, not Wooru income; if residents must pay no delivery surcharge, the supplier, sponsor or association funds it explicitly. Illustrative arithmetic only: 40 households × 10 kg of rice at ₹52/kg plus ₹1,200 shared transport is ₹55/kg at pickup; against a comparable hypothetical ₹65/kg, each household saves ₹100 before any doorstep delivery charge. No real supplier quote or sponsor commitment is implied.

Formulas, exclusions, and how to interpret the estimate

Meta = marketing count x marketing rate + utility count x utility rate + authentication count x authentication rate + service count x service rate. Counts are delivered and already net of exemptions. Each preset assumes uniform service usage and applies a maximum 1,000 service messages/number exemption before deriving its chargeable counts. Edited counts are not automatically allocated across sellers.

360dialog = numbers x EUR monthly list fee x EUR/INR + entered extra fees. Wati = workspaces x entered INR fee + entered extra fees. Twilio = (inbound + outbound) x USD handling fee x USD/INR + entered extra fees. Direct provider fee = 0; this is not zero integration cost.

Monthly subtotal = Meta + provider + backend + support. Monthly tax = subtotal x tax %. Monthly estimate = subtotal + tax. First-month economic estimate = monthly estimate + setup x (1 + tax %). For Wati, quarterly base subscription cash = workspace fee x workspace count x 3 before taxes; monthly equivalents are not a cash-flow forecast.

All routes use the same illustrative Meta pass-through and operating allowance for comparability, not because actual contracts or engineering needs are identical. 12 / TEAM DECISION AND PROOF

Prove the savings and the payer before building the network.

One manual buying-circle test

One society, one essentials basket, two comparable supplier quotes and pickup first. Supplier invoices each household.

Go / no-go gates

Real net savings, reliable fulfillment, resident opt-in and a sponsor-funded association contract that covers Wooru's costs.

Measure real value

Repeat participation, fulfilled orders, complaints, supplier performance, sponsor renewal and actual cost per community.

Next: validate the idea, not build it. Add produce, delivery APIs, AI or paid WhatsApp only when savings, operations and funding are proven.

Decision areaQuestion to answer before implementation
Buying circleDoes a comparable essentials basket cost less after packing, transport and quality issues? Can a supplier invoice and fulfill household orders at the aggregate price?
IncomeWill a sponsor fund the association, and will the association sign a separate Wooru contract? Does the fee cover the full operating cost rather than just the illustrative chat model?
