01 / Wooru + WhatsApp · decision briefing · researched 12 November 2026

One Wooru number.
Local orders, from WhatsApp.

Start with a Wooru-owned community concierge number for browsing, ordering and updates. Wooru keeps the order; WhatsApp is a familiar channel. Seller-owned Business numbers are a separate optional expansion, not a prerequisite for the pilot.

Pilot: one Wooru-owned number

Residents use ordinary WhatsApp to ask across participating food sellers. Sellers manage their menus and orders in Wooru; they do not have to connect a Business number. Later, eligible sellers could optionally connect their own number via Coexistence.

No: mirroring existing society groups

Group chats are not synced through Coexistence. Meta's Groups API is a separate product: Official Business Accounts only, max 8 participants, and not for Business-app numbers. Unofficial WhatsApp Web bots are not an option.

02 / How it works today

Orders get lost in the society group.

  • Menus disappear in a busy group; “two please” is not a recorded order.
  • Sellers reconcile chats by hand while Wooru’s structured menus go unseen.

The opportunity: bring the Wooru order record to a familiar chat, without scraping groups.

Large groups mix orders with notices and greetings; manual notebook tallying can miss or duplicate orders. Wooru already has proper menus and orders, but residents do not always open another app.

03 / Platform rules, not opinions

One Wooru number first. Seller numbers only if useful later.

Personal app

What residents use. No API to read their chats or groups, and none is needed: they deliberately message Wooru.

Business app

What our sellers already use. Free and manual. On its own it has no automation API.

Cloud API · pilot

One Wooru-owned number: webhooks, interactive buttons and lists, templates. Outbound fees depend on message category and current rules.

Coexistence · later

Eligible sellers may connect their own Business app number to the Cloud API for 1:1 chats. This is not needed for a Wooru-number pilot.

04 / One-time setup

The seller joins Wooru. Residents link once.

  • Priya opts in and publishes her menu in Wooru. No Business number connection needed.
  • Rahul links once by approving an expiring request in his signed-in app.

One Wooru-owned number for the pilot; seller-number Coexistence is later.

Priya may share a menu link into a group herself, never automatically. Rahul can reject an unexpected or expired link, request a new one, unlink or switch accounts. Official messaging access, opt-in and identity-link design remain pilot gates.

05 / WhatsApp → Wooru

A menu sent to Wooru becomes a draft, on purpose.

  • Priya texts the Wooru-owned number; missing details are asked for, not guessed.
  • She reviews a private draft and taps Publish in Wooru.

No private chat or group post becomes a public menu automatically.

Published menus follow the app’s existing moderation, capacity and cutoff rules. Chat-based seller drafting is optional in the pilot, not a prerequisite.

06 / WhatsApp → Wooru → WhatsApp

Browse across sellers. Review, then confirm.

  • Choose a live menu from your own society with a list, not guesswork.
  • Review seller, total, delivery and payment; tap Place order explicitly.
  • Wooru checks capacity and confirms only after an order record exists.

WhatsApp is the window. Wooru is the order system.

The existing place_mcn_preorder rules enforce membership, cutoff, price and capacity. Show Checking order until the write succeeds. Changed terms or sold-out require a new review. On timeout, check My Orders before retrying. The same record appears in the seller dashboard and the resident’s My Orders.

07 / Both directions

Ask Wooru for your orders. Get reliable updates.

  • “My orders” returns only Rahul’s own records across participating sellers.
  • Priya updates an order in Wooru; Rahul receives its latest status in chat.

Blue ticks show a message was read—not that the food was delivered.

Cancellation needs confirmation and a clear outcome; seller cancellation needs a reason and next action. Outside the 24-hour window, updates need approved utility templates. Wooru remains the source of truth.

Formulas, exclusions, and how to interpret the estimate

Meta = marketing count x marketing rate + utility count x utility rate + authentication count x authentication rate + service count x service rate. Counts are delivered and already net of exemptions. Each preset assumes uniform service usage and applies a maximum 1,000 service messages/number exemption before deriving its chargeable counts. Edited counts are not automatically allocated across sellers.

360dialog = numbers x EUR monthly list fee x EUR/INR + entered extra fees. Wati = workspaces x entered INR fee + entered extra fees. Twilio = (inbound + outbound) x USD handling fee x USD/INR + entered extra fees. Direct provider fee = 0; this is not zero integration cost.

Monthly subtotal = Meta + provider + backend + support. Monthly tax = subtotal x tax %. Monthly estimate = subtotal + tax. First-month economic estimate = monthly estimate + setup x (1 + tax %). For Wati, quarterly base subscription cash = workspace fee x workspace count x 3 before taxes; monthly equivalents are not a cash-flow forecast.

All routes use the same illustrative Meta pass-through and operating allowance for comparability, not because actual contracts or engineering needs are identical. Pilot: seller count does not multiply the one Wooru-owned number. Later seller-number Coexistence: count eligible connected numbers separately. Wati may use its own message rate card; reflect differences in its extra-fees input. No volume tiers, automatic FEP detection, cross-border/authentication uplifts, ads, AI/OCR, payment processing, phone rental, or unentered vendor charges are included. Partner arrangements and seller eligibility remain unverified.

Blank, invalid, or unknown required numeric values stop calculation. Enter a known zero explicitly. Displayed zeros in extra-fee defaults are labeled exclusions, not evidence that no such charges apply. No “cheapest eligible route” is inferred.

A chat adapter in front of Wooru, not a second order system.

Identity first

A signed-in Wooru member links the chat; the server scopes every request to their society.

One source of truth

An order is confirmed only when Wooru’s transaction succeeds, never on an AI guess.

Safe delivery

Verify webhooks, handle retries idempotently and respect messaging opt-outs.

Identity & tenancy

  • Link WhatsApp ID ↔ Wooru user via in-app confirmation.
  • Support business-scoped IDs when no phone number is shared.
  • Community resolved on the server, never from message text.
  • Service-role keys never bypass resident rules.

Reliability

  • Webhooks can arrive late, batched or retried for up to 7 days, so make every action idempotent.
  • Ignore seller echoes to avoid loops.
  • Edited or deleted messages never silently change orders.

Privacy & policy

  • Opt-in, opt-out, retention and deletion.
  • No resident chats sent to third-party AI/OCR without a vetted agreement.
  • No private chat is ever made public.

Operations

  • Public HTTPS endpoint (not local Docker).
  • Resident unlink, opt-out and seller participation controls.
  • Monitor one pilot number; human fallback and template management.

Ready to reuse in Wooru

Transactional food pre-orders (place_mcn_preorder: membership, cut-off, locks, server prices), order statuses, the notification and web-push pipeline, and Edge Functions.

New work needed

WhatsApp identity-link and unlink flow, webhook and outbound sender, conversation state, delegated-actor calls into the RPCs, templates, seller opt-in, admin monitoring and explicit pending/failure UX. Seller-number connection is later. Service bookings need a lifecycle first.

10 / Decision

Prove one Wooru-number food-order journey first.

Recommendation

Evaluate 360dialog or direct Meta for one Wooru-owned API number. Quote the actual pilot separately. Evaluate seller-number Coexistence only after the community-wide flow proves valuable.

Pilot scope

One society, 2–3 food sellers, one Wooru number. Link residents, browse menus, review orders and track status.

Go / no-go gates

Official onboarding and a written quote; linked identity, opt-out and failed-order UX; seller consent and privacy review.

Seller menu drafting by chat and human-initiated sharing to existing groups are optional. No group sync, bookings or AI parsing in the first pilot. Seller-number Coexistence needs separate approval and eligibility checks later.

Do residents need anything new?

They use ordinary WhatsApp but need a Wooru account and a one-time signed-in link to the Wooru number. They can reject an unexpected link or unlink later. Their other chats and groups are never visible to Wooru.

Why not just read the society group?

Coexistence does not sync groups. The Groups API needs an Official Business Account, caps groups at 8 participants and excludes Business-app numbers. Unofficial WhatsApp Web automation risks bans, session compromise and privacy breaches. Sellers can still share a Wooru menu link in groups manually.

Is it free if the resident messages first?

Meta doesn't charge for inbound messages. Outbound charges depend on category, market and current free tiers. Providers add per-number or per-message fees. Don't assume everything inside the 24-hour window is free.

Can we use our Supabase service-role key for chat orders?

Not on its own. The order RPC relies on the signed-in user's identity. A chat integration needs a secure delegated-actor design that keeps membership, pricing, capacity and ownership checks intact.

What happens to old chats and the seller's phone?

Nothing happens to the seller's Business app in the Wooru-number pilot. If a seller later chooses Coexistence, up to 6 months of history may optionally sync, but old messages must never create orders; onboarding can unlink companion devices, and seller eligibility needs checking.
